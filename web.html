Health & Awareness

Drugs.

Drug addiction is a disease that affects a person's brain and behavior, leading to an inability to control drug use — here's what to know.

What Is a Drug?

A drug is a substance that can change the way the body or brain functions. Some drugs are used as medicines to prevent or treat diseases, while other substances can be harmful when misused.

Drugs can affect a person's mood, thinking, behavior, and physical health.

Addiction of Drugs

Drug addiction is a condition in which a person continues to use a drug despite harmful effects. Repeated drug use can affect the brain's reward and decision-making systems.

How dependency can develop

  • Repeated use can lead to changes in the brain.
  • A person may develop a strong desire to use the substance.
  • Stopping may become difficult without appropriate support.
  • Drug use can affect school, work, relationships, and health.
Remember: Addiction is a health issue, and people experiencing problems with substance use can need professional help and support.

Government Act

Governments use laws and public-health programmes to control harmful drugs, reduce illegal drug trafficking, and protect communities.

Drug control can include

  • Laws controlling illegal drugs and their distribution.
  • Action against illegal trafficking and production.
  • Public awareness and prevention programmes.
  • Support for people affected by drug dependence.

De-addiction Centres

De-addiction centres provide support to people who are experiencing problems with drug or substance use.

Support may include

  • Assessment by trained professionals.
  • Medical care when appropriate.
  • Counselling and psychological support.
  • Family and social support.
  • Recovery and rehabilitation programmes.
Getting help matters. If someone is struggling with substance use, speaking with a trusted adult or qualified healthcare professional can be an important first step.
