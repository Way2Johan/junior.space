Overview

Animal tissue

Animal tissue is a group of animal cells that have similar structures and functions. There are four basic types of animal tissue:

  • Epithelial tissue
  • Connective tissue
  • Muscular tissue
  • Nervous tissue
Type 1

Epithelial tissue

Epithelial tissue is the covering or protective tissue of the animal body. It covers most organs and cavities within the body, serving as a protective layer and forming the lining of body surfaces and cavities, as well as the outer layer of skin.

Key characteristics:

  • Cellularity — made up of tightly packed cells with minimal extracellular material.
  • Polarity — cells have distinct top (apical) and bottom (basal) surfaces; the apical surface faces the body's exterior or an internal cavity, while the basal surface attaches to underlying connective tissue via the basement membrane.
  • Avascularity — lacks blood vessels and relies on diffusion from underlying connective tissue for nutrients and waste removal.
  • Regeneration — has a high capacity to regenerate, important for tissues under constant wear, like skin and intestinal linings.
Type 2

Connective tissue

Connective tissue is one of the four fundamental tissue types in the body, playing a crucial role in supporting, connecting, and separating other tissues and organs. It's set apart by its abundant extracellular matrix, unlike epithelial, muscle, or nervous tissue.

Key features:

  • Extracellular matrix — fewer cells, and a large amount of matrix made of fibers (collagen, elastin, reticular fibers) and ground substance (a gel-like mix of water, proteins, and glycosaminoglycans).
  • Vascularity — varies by type: bone is highly vascular, while cartilage has no blood vessels at all.
  • Diverse cell types — includes fibroblasts (which produce fibers), adipocytes (fat cells), chondrocytes (cartilage cells), osteocytes (bone cells), and blood cells.
Type 3

Muscular tissue

Muscular tissue is responsible for producing movement by contracting and relaxing. It's made of muscle fibers and comes in three types — skeletal, smooth, and cardiac — each playing a distinct role, from voluntary movement to internal organ control and heart function.

Type 4

Nervous tissue

Nervous tissue makes up the central and peripheral nervous systems, enabling communication between different parts of the body. It consists of neurons, which transmit electrical signals, and glial cells, which support, protect, and nourish the neurons. Nervous tissue is essential for coordinating body functions, processing information, and responding to stimuli.
